Privacy Policy

Last updated 13 November 2026 · Applies to the Uncemo app and uncemo.com

Uncemo is a personal assistant that, with your permission, reads your calendar and email so it can act on your behalf. That only works if you can trust what we do with your data. This notice says exactly what we collect, why, who else sees it, how long we keep it, and how to get it or delete it. It is written to be read, not skimmed.

Who we are

Uncemo ("we", "us") provides the Uncemo app and uncemo.com and is the data controller for the personal data described here. We are based in the United Kingdom. For anything about your data, write to privacy@uncemo.com; we answer within a month, usually much sooner.

Why we use it, and the legal basis

UK data protection law (the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018) says we need a lawful basis for each use of your data. These are ours:

  • Providing the service you asked for (performance of a contract): your account, the conversation, memories, to-dos, reminders, calendar and email features, push notifications, the morning brief, and the daily allowance.
  • Your consent: connecting Google or Microsoft, sharing the calendars and contacts on your phone, adding your mobile number for calls, and enabling notifications. Each has its own switch or button in the app and you can withdraw consent at any time by turning it off or disconnecting; the data we hold from it is deleted as described below.
  • Our legitimate interests, balanced against yours: keeping the service secure and available, preventing abuse, applying usage limits, reading feedback to fix problems, keeping short-lived backups, and telling waitlist members when they can join. You can object to any of these (see "Your rights").
  • Legal obligations: keeping records we are required to keep, and answering lawful requests from authorities, which we would tell you about unless the law prevents it.

Google and Microsoft data

Connecting Google or Microsoft is optional and separate from signing in. When you connect, the provider asks you to grant these permissions, and you can revoke them at any time from the Account tab in the app, from your Google account's third-party access page, or from your Microsoft account's privacy settings.

  • Calendar (Google Calendar, Outlook calendar). Used to read your events and to create, move or delete events when you ask.
  • Google Tasks. Your to-do list in the app and your default Google Tasks list are kept the same: adds, edits, ticks and deletions travel both ways.
  • Mail (Gmail, Outlook). Gmail is a separate add-on you can decline: Google Calendar and Tasks work without it. Used to search and read messages you ask about, to check your inbox for new mail worth telling you about, to create and update drafts in your Drafts folder, to send a draft when you tap Send on it in the app, and to archive a message when you ask. The assistant never sends email on its own: every send is your tap, on the draft's card or in Gmail or Outlook. It never deletes email. New mail's sender, subject and preview line are also used to suggest to-dos ("Pay the deposit by Friday") in the To-do tab; nothing goes on your list until you tap Add, and you can switch this off under Notifications in Account.

Email and calendar content is read when a request needs it and is passed to our AI provider to produce the answer (see "Who else sees your data"). We do not keep copies of your mailbox. What we do keep: a short summary of recent inbox mail (sender, subject, first line) for the Today tab and suggestions, the ids of mail we have already checked, the drafts the assistant wrote for you (they appear as cards in your chat), and whatever the assistant quoted into its replies. All of it is deleted with your account. Your access tokens are stored encrypted on our server.

Uncemo's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In plain terms: Google data is used only to provide the features you see in the app, is never used for advertising, is never sold, and is never used to train AI models. The same rules apply to Microsoft data.

Your phone's calendars and contacts

Two switches in Account, both off until you turn them on. "Calendars on this phone" sends the next two weeks of events from the calendars on your phone (title, time, place and calendar name) so the assistant sees them next to Google and Outlook; it can read them but not change them. "Contacts" sends names, email addresses and phone numbers so that "email Ada" or "text Jake" finds the right person; nothing else from a contact is sent. The phone refreshes both while the switch is on. Turning a switch off deletes our copy at once.

Phone calls

Optional. You add your mobile number in Account and confirm it with a code we text you; then calls from that number reach your assistant. Twilio carries the call and turns your speech into text and the assistant's replies into speech, live, during the call. We do not record calls and Twilio does not keep the audio for us; what you said and what the assistant answered are kept as text in your chat, like any other message. Call minutes are counted to apply a daily and weekly allowance. Remove the number in Account and calls stop at once.

How the AI is used, and automated decisions

Every reply is written by an AI model run by Anthropic (Claude). To write it, the model receives your message, the recent conversation, your name and "about you" note, your memories, your open to-dos, and whatever calendar, email or contact data the request needs. The model can also save a memory about you when you tell it something worth keeping; you see every memory in the Memory tab and can delete any of them.

A few things happen without you asking, so you know: a fast model sorts new inbox mail into "worth a notification" or not, suggests to-dos from new mail, and files your memories under headings like work, people or health. Another picks which size of model answers each message. None of this makes decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on you, and nothing is sent, booked or deleted on your behalf without your request or your tap. You can turn off inbox alerts and to-do suggestions under Notifications in Account.

If you ask the assistant to look something up online, the search runs through Anthropic's web search; your question, not your data, is what gets searched.

Who else sees your data

We use a small number of service providers ("processors") who handle data only on our instructions and under a contract. All of them are in the United States, which is why the next section matters.

  • Anthropic (Claude API), United States: receives the content of your request, relevant email, calendar or contact text, your memories and to-dos, so it can generate the assistant's reply. Anthropic does not use API data to train its models.
  • OpenAI, United States: receives the audio of voice notes to transcribe them, and nothing else.
  • Google and Microsoft: receive the requests needed to read and change your calendar, tasks and mail when you connect those accounts.
  • Supabase, United States: handles sign-in and stores your email address and sign-in method.
  • Railway, United States (Virginia): hosts our server, your data and its backups.
  • Expo, United States: delivers push notifications (the notification text passes through their service) and app updates.
  • Resend, United States: sends sign-in codes and waitlist emails.
  • Twilio, United States: carries phone calls to the assistant and the verification text, and converts speech to text and back during a call.
  • Apple and Google (app stores), and RevenueCat, United States: if paid plans launch and you buy one, the store takes the payment and RevenueCat processes the receipt. We never see your card details.

We never sell your data, never share it with advertisers, never show you ads, and never use your data to train AI models. We do not use analytics or tracking SDKs in the app.

Transfers outside the UK

Our server and our providers are in the United States, so your data leaves the UK. We rely on the safeguards UK law allows for this: each provider is bound by data protection terms that include the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses or the ICO's International Data Transfer Agreement, and where a provider is certified under the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework we rely on that too. Email privacy@uncemo.com if you want details of the safeguard for a particular provider.

Where your data lives

Each account has its own separate database file on our server. There is no shared table of everyone's messages. Access tokens from Google and Microsoft are encrypted at rest. A backup of the databases is taken every night and kept for 7 days, then deleted.

How long we keep it

  • Your account, conversation, memories, to-dos, reminders and the cards in your chat: for as long as you have an account, or until you delete the item.
  • Google and Microsoft tokens: until you disconnect the account or delete yours.
  • Phone calendar and contact copies: deleted the moment you turn the switch off.
  • Inbox summaries for the Today tab: replaced at each check; ids of mail already checked: 30 days; to-do suggestions you have not answered: 14 days.
  • Call transcripts: with your chat; call minute counts: 7 days; verification codes: 10 minutes.
  • Usage counts: 90 days. Feedback: until you delete your account. Diagnostic logs: a short period set by our host, typically days.
  • Backups: 7 days, so a deleted account can linger in a backup for up to a week before it is gone everywhere.
  • Waitlist emails: until we launch or you ask us to remove them.

Your controls: export and delete

Account tab, then "Download my data" gives you everything we hold about you as one file, in a readable format you can keep or move elsewhere.

Account tab, then "Delete my account and data" immediately removes your account, your Google and Microsoft tokens, your conversation history, memories, to-dos, reminders, phone calendar and contact copies, your mobile number, feedback, push tokens and usage counts, and deletes your sign-in record at Supabase. We also revoke our access at Google; for Microsoft, remove Uncemo from your Microsoft account's privacy settings if you want to be sure. It cannot be undone. You can also email privacy@uncemo.com and we will do it for you within 7 days.

You can forget individual memories from the Memory tab, disconnect Google or Microsoft, remove your phone number, and turn off every switch, all without deleting your account.

Security

All traffic uses HTTPS. Access tokens are encrypted on disk. Sign-in uses one-time codes or Google and Apple identity; we never see or store a password. Our server only ever acts on a request carrying your signed session token, and incoming phone calls are checked against Twilio's signature before anything happens. If we ever discover a breach that puts you at risk, we will tell you and the ICO without undue delay.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you have the right to: access your data; have it corrected; have it erased; restrict or object to how we use it, including anything based on our legitimate interests; take it with you in a portable format; and withdraw consent at any time without affecting what was done before. The app covers most of this directly (Download my data, Delete my account, the switches). For anything else, email privacy@uncemo.com. We reply within one month and do not charge for this.

If you are in the EU or elsewhere, you have equivalent rights under your local law and can use the same address.

Children

Uncemo is not for anyone under 16, and you confirm you are 16 or older when you first open the app. We do not knowingly collect data from children. If you believe a child is using Uncemo, tell us at privacy@uncemo.com and we will delete the account.

Complaints

If you are unhappy with how we handle your data, please tell us first at privacy@uncemo.com so we can put it right. You also have the right to complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO): ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint, or 0303 123 1113.

Changes

If this notice changes in a way that matters, we will show it in the app before it takes effect and ask you to read it again. The date at the top always shows the current version.
